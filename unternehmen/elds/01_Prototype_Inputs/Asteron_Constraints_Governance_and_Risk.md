Constraints, governance and risk
Entirely fictional. Case snapshot: August 31, 2026. Synthetic organizational evidence for a hackathon prototype.
Policy status
These are fictional Asteron pilot rules, not a statement of current law. Privacy, cross-border
processing, customer contracts, employment implications and any sector-specific obligations
must be reviewed by the relevant legal and security owners before real deployment. The
hackathon uses synthetic data only.
Customer data and security
Collect only necessary fields. Remove secrets, access tokens, sensitive attachments and
unnecessary personal data before ingestion. Tenant-level access controls must apply during
retrieval and generation. A user must never see another customer's material. Use an approved
isolated environment with contractual restrictions on training from customer content. Keep
encrypted storage and transfer, role-based access, and an audit trail. Pilot caches expire after 30
days; approved audit records after 90 days, subject to a documented legal hold.
Technology and integration limits
CaseHarbor exports arrive nightly. CRM entitlements can therefore be a day old. The acquired
product space lacks a production connector. The prototype must simulate authorization rather
than claim live permission enforcement. Production connector work needs separate acceptance
tests. No production action, customer email, billing adjustment, entitlement change or data replay
is allowed from the hackathon.
Operational limits
Pilot incremental spend ceiling: EUR 60,000 over eight weeks. Preserve existing customer
commitments. Allow at most two hours per participating agent per week for training and
feedback. Use reversible changes and a documented rollback. Measure extra review and
correction time, not only gross time saved.

Human at the Center
Design around customers who need accurate, understandable help and employees who need
usable support and room to exercise judgment. Include multilingual users and less experienced
agents in testing. Offer a route to a person and a way to challenge an answer. Avoid turning
quality monitoring into covert employee surveillance.
Human in Command
The head of Support owns service outcomes, pilot scope, budget, capacity reassignment and
stop decisions. Product owners authorize technical reference changes. Security controls access
and incident escalation. Customer administrators authorize changes in their own environments.
Accountability cannot be delegated to a model.
Human in the Loop
A trained agent reviews customer-facing technical advice, checks applicability and confirms
high-impact actions with the authorized owner. Human approval is required for data replay,
security or permission changes, commercial commitments and uncertain cross-product remedies.
Review must include enough source context and time to be meaningful.
Human on the Loop
After separate validation, reversible internal tags or background issue clustering may run without
per-item approval within explicit boundaries. A named operations owner reviews exceptions daily
and samples at least 10% of outputs weekly. Provide audit logs and immediate disable/rollback.
Stop on any cross-tenant exposure or unauthorized action; pause a capability if material error
exceeds 5% in the weekly sample.
Choosing the work design
Human-led work remains appropriate for empathy, relationship repair, business tradeoffs and
accountability. AI-led / autonomous work requires bounded tasks, low consequence of error and
effective intervention. Human-AI collaboration combines machine assistance with active human
judgment. Non-AI organizational change may be sufficient where a policy, ownership, staffing or
process decision is the real constraint. These are design choices, not a maturity ladder.
